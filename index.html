01 — Past

Where I came
from

COMMUNITY

I grew up as a first-generation Cypriot with Belarusian roots. There were three points in my life that directed me towards industrial design which were my first job, a conversation with a soldier and my commander as a role model. During school I studied triple science. Nothing pointed me towards the direction of industrial design. Then in my senior year I started a part time job in a VR center for kids. That job taught me more than I expected. Not from the equipment, but from the people. Navigating overprotective parents, being patient with young children, and working with kids with disabilities taught me early that the people using a product matter just as much as the product itself. I didn't have the vocabulary for it then, but that was my first real experience of user empathy.

After school came 13 months of military service as a tanker. Long guard shifts gave me time I didn't ask for but needed, staring into nothing, thinking about where I was going. Two people shaped that thinking. A conversation with a senior soldier who had no plan as a young adult and regretting not having a five year plan, served as a warning that made me evaluate where I was going. And my commander, who led not through fear but through fairness and truth. Watching him made me understand what it meant to be a good leader, despite the strict hierarchy, he treated every person under him as an individual worth listening to. That's something I've carried into every team I've worked in since.

But what really changed me in the army was working on the tanks with my unit. Even with a language barrier the work became a form of communication. The labor of keeping something complex running built a community around it. I fell in love with making things work with my hands. That feeling of working on something bigger than yourself, alongside people who became more than colleagues, is what pointed me toward Industrial Design. Not a five-year plan, but a sense of community.

More than a Design. A Community.
02 — Professional Identity

Who I Am
as a designer

What is it like to work with me.

Growing up between two cultures without fully belonging to either could make you question your identity. I never did. Not because the gap wasn't there, it was, but because I always found belonging somewhere else. In school, in the army, in games. What I noticed early is that community doesn't come from shared background. It comes from shared experience.

The language barrier was with me long before the army. I have lived in Cyprus my entire life and still cannot speak the language fluently. Then I spent thirteen months where it was the only form of communication around me. That experience taught me something I have carried into every team since: I can tell when someone's voice isn't being heard because mine often wasn't. It also taught me that actions communicate what words can't. How you show up, what you make, how you treat the work, that tells people who you are. I bring that into how I design and how I collaborate.

The other environment that shaped me more than I realized is video games. I loved them, the freedom you feel in a virtual world you can truly create anything, and I believe they have strongly influenced how I approach problem solving. But looking back I definitely see some side effects of my near addiction. I was completely consumed. Which affected me negatively, but now I have learnt from it. Whenever I approach a design I always think about how it can negatively affect a user, which was a main realization in CBL 1.

How do you design an experience that makes someone feel genuinely part of something? That question is where my design identity lives right now.

03 — Vision

What Design
can do

Immersion in video games.
Design can build community. But I want to develop a medium.

My vision centers on the untapped potential of videogames as tools for genuine education and personal development. The direction I want to take to approach this is through immersion, which isn't just about high-fidelity graphics; it requires both technical innovation and ethical engagement. It is about building systems that connect people to something real.

When players are genuinely immersed, whether it is due to the quality of the technology or the feelings that the game makes you feel, space is created for development. I want to push the boundaries of how games bridge the physical and digital worlds to create this new form of development. By designing experiences that don't just entertain, but actively shape how we communicate, learn, and grow.

I do not know yet where this leads. The goal for now is to develop the tools — technical, conceptual, collaborative — to follow the question wherever it goes.

04 — Present

What I am

How have my expertise areas developed

Creativity & Aesthetics

To me, Creativity and Aesthetics is the core of what industrial design is. It isn't just about the final prototype or making something look "nice." It is the entire process of getting there. During my first year I have noticed that C&A is a subjective EA. This is due to when we discussed about different game characters in our CBL1 project, there were all kinds of different opinions on what looked and felt good. This caused me to adapt the project from what I believed looked good to what the user believed. Since in the end I design for the user not the designer. This viewpoint will carry on into my vision since I'm not designing for myself, I am trying to create a more immersive experience for the users.

Creativity for me covers the process and the creative problem solving of how I approach the design. This was done by following different techniques and methodologies in creating my project for CBL1. Creating brainstorms on what we wanted our game to be, sketching out my own ideas of the boardgame and playable characters, prototyping a physical product with cardboard and styrofoam that illustrated our idea to our assessor. What I thought worked really well while working on my sketches was writing down my idea and reflections directly on the sketch. This made it a lot easier to be able to explain my process for myself and peers. On the other hand, I struggled to recognize that low fidelity prototypes were just that "low-fidelity prototypes". I found myself putting in a lot of time and effort into a direction that as a team we decided to deviate from. In the future I plan to keep more contact with my team to avoid developing a direction we do not follow by the end.

While Aesthetics for me explains that process through human senses. The design should be able to show the design process. In our CBL1 project we wanted to create a boardgame that would stop the user from looking down at the board. Instead to look at the person you are playing with. This design choice influenced the board game to change from a typical flat style to the globelike shape that causes you to naturally raise your eyes. I really enjoyed the whole idea of designing around a principle like avoiding looking down at the game. In the future I want to follow this structure where I follow certain rules like design manifestos.

User & Society

Initially, I thought the 'User and Society' expertise area just meant taking the user's point of view into account during a product's development through questionnaires and interviews. Looking back, it was a very closed-minded view of the influence the user has on the product. Currently I would define it as "users' effect on design and design's effect on society".

CBL project 1 was where I got this understanding of U&S. Through the course Human-Centred Design I started learning design based on a user-centred perspective especially using RTDP. I created personas for our game that we would act out to get a better understanding of what adjustments we should make. In our game this made us remember how memorable the play figures in games are — you would constantly fight over who gets to be the cooler-looking one. Through just acting out as the individual I was able to give a perspective on the design that I didn't think of before. Now this is something I constantly do. I love playing out as different people during the design process to really get all perspectives, especially enjoying playing devil's advocate to see what they might love and hate. This viewpoint made us change the final design to also have a team mode so that people who might feel that they wouldn't enjoy playing as much have help from a teammate. Additionally it changed the style of our questions from trivia to more fun and interactive ways of play. Now I see how important design is from the user's perspective, but also from the opposite user — it can give possible insight that could change your design.

Furthermore, I found interviews to be the most effective way of gathering user-centred data, especially when the format was open-ended questions. In our game a user study was done and then a following interview was conducted. Through this interview we were able to determine how we had introduced bias into our game — that the questions centred around Europe were a lot harder than the ones outside of the area. This showed a blind-spot during our design process. Since we were all European, we naturally made the harder questions in our region. This interview question about game difficulty made me understand better how biases can be easily introduced in games. Follow-up research showed that in our game there were multiple ways we were introducing bias, such as: drawing out the different regions, question difficulty, and figurine classes. How figurines introduced bias was the most interesting to me. We had playable characters based on the countries' national animals, illustrating their culture in a respectable way. If this were done distastefully it could introduce bias, but more interestingly for me was the game mechanics side — introducing a class-based system in a country-based board game, meaning the different characters had different powers. These powers would directly influence whether you would win or lose. This made me realise how power scaling could influence the perception of a country — for example, if a character was weaker, it could reflect my own personal bias or even have a systemic effect where users start seeing a country negatively because they are weak in-game. This made me realise that in many class-based video games where specific countries are named, they can have an influence on how society perceives them. Moreover, whose fault is it — is it the fault of the user who thinks of these negative stereotypes, or is it the designer's fault for introducing the conversation?

User and Society has made me deeply aware of my influence on society's views. Moving forward, my goal is to be much better at identifying and avoiding biases in my products.

Technology & Realization

Technology and realization is my weakest EA and it is the one I plan to work on the most in my second term. Right now, I define it as how I introduce electronics and mechanics into my projects. Which for my first project I didn't have any prior knowledge on how to introduce it.

Currently I haven't introduced Arduino beyond trying to incorporate them into my creative programming game. This was done by initially planning out to use an Arduino with buttons as a controller and starting to code the controller. Unfortunately, I was not able to use it for my project due to running out of time. This was a time period where I was studying remotely and did not have access to Arduino and electronics. From now on I see that working on technology is something that I couldn't do remotely and, in the future, will have to plan around it.

Lastly, I would like to focus more on the realization of this EA that for me is categorized as mechanics. To be able to develop this skill I want to develop 3D modeling skills to be able to 3D print the mechanics necessary for my projects.

Business & Entrepreneurship

Business and Entrepreneurship is an expertise area that is not focused on the first year. This is why I felt like I have not developed enough in this EA. This reflection was made after looking into the rubric and seeing that I didn't consider any of the subcategories in B&E towards my projects. Since I didn't take them into account before I decided to look into my project from this new perspective.

This is where I noticed some development towards B&E. Firstly, in regard to market analysis and identifying competition. I didn't do any more research than just looking into whether my game for CBL1 existed or not. Instead of this, from now on I want to approach the market from a perspective of "What is missing, and what I can add to it". I believe this is a better approach for making a product because it focuses on innovation rather than trying to reinvent the wheel. Secondly, I started getting the idea of customer value in CBL1. We got user feedback in regard to the packaging of our game, that a game so large and frustrating to move could discourage users from buying/playing the game. This made me start thinking more critically regarding what the net perceived benefit of the user is. This is why I tried making the packaging more accessible for users by decreasing the size of when it is in the box, because if we decrease the size which caused nuisance, there will be an increased benefit for the customer. This was done by changing the shape of our design from a pre-built globe to a puzzle that forms into a spherical polyhedron which becomes the game board.

Business and Entrepreneurship for me has become why and how I make design choices towards commercial use or innovation for societal development. I have already decided to develop these skills due to the lack of official courses by joining first-years' weekend committee as treasurer. Through this committee I want to gain a better understanding on how to create a product that people will buy, in other words creating a product for commercial use. This will be done while developing skills in planning, budgeting, and communication. I believe this will be a good baseline for my first course in B&E (Design Innovation), where I want to build on it by learning how to analyze the market for potential value.

Overall, in my vision I see how commercial thinking matters because immersive digital-physical products only reach people if they're viable to produce and sell, not just well-designed.

Math, Data & Computing

Math, Data and Computing is an EA that I did not focus on in the 1st year. Additionally, it is an area that I haven't developed significantly enough in my CBL. I have developed them through my core courses Foundation of Data Analytics and Creative Programming. These courses were my introduction into the world of programming. Currently I define MDC as, "How I can manipulate math and code to create solutions for practical use?". This links into my future vision because if I want to create immersive digital-physical experiences, code is the medium I'll use to build them. Moreover, it gives me a new way of developing my design from a data driven angle, which would be useful when developing systems and testing if it is even viable.

In an assignment for Creative Programming I created a Dance Dance Revolution game that really helped me in my development of game design and programming skills. Through this game I had to manage data collected to be able to calculate a final score based on your accuracy, streak and points, as well as managing how the interaction feels as interactable software. I didn't anticipate how important visual changes during interaction were. Initially when an arrow is pressed in game and you collect the point, it didn't feel like you collected the point. What really helped was adding a highlight based on what button was pressed and slowly fading out arrows that were correctly pressed.

Collaboration

As a teammate I would describe myself as passionate and considerate.As a teammate I would describe myself as passionate and considerate. When working in a group I find myself getting invested in the project, bouncing ideas off my teammates and really enjoying the inclusion of everyone's input. I also make an effort to include everyone, making sure each person has a chance to express how they want to take the idea forward.

In CBL1 we didn't have formal roles assigned, they emerged naturally. Mine ended up being sketching out ideas, finalising the ethics of the product, and assisting with prototyping closer to the end of the project.

One of my biggest contributions was pushing for the redesign of our game board from a pre-built globe to a flat-pack puzzle that forms a spherical polyhedron. This was my idea and it was a difficult change to introduce to my groupmates due to how late in the project it was made. We had already built two prototypes, so this felt like a step back to them. I believed the change was important because it added a depth to the design that wasn't there before, so I pushed for it even with the short deadline. Looking back, this urgency was avoidable. If we had gathered user feedback earlier, we would have found out sooner that our game lacked in quality of life. This is why for project 2 I plan on building a functional prototype in the first quarter to catch these issues earlier.

My other main contribution was owning the ethical side of the project. It was during this process, while conducting interviews and finalising the ethics, that I identified how we were introducing bias into our game through question difficulty, figurine power scaling, and regional representation.

Something I am not proud of is that midway through the project I burnt out and disengaged for about a week. This made my teammates frustrated and led to a direct confrontation. I found that this kind of direct approach works best for me. Because of this I now inform my teammates in advance how I respond to criticism and pressure, rather than waiting for it to become a problem.
